Hole and shaft fits explained

Clearance, transition and interference fits in plain terms, with the H7 fits you will meet most often.

A fit describes how a shaft and a hole go together: with a gap, with a slight grip, or with a firm press. The ISO 286 system expresses each fit as a pair of tolerance codes, such as H7/g6. The capital letter is the hole, and the small letter is the shaft.

Why most fits start with H7

In the hole-basis system, the hole is made to an H tolerance, which starts exactly at the nominal size and goes upwards. Holes are harder to make to odd sizes than shafts, so standard reamers and gauges are made for H holes, and the shaft is varied to get the fit you want. H7 is the most common hole grade for precision work.

Three kinds of fit

  • Clearance: the shaft is always smaller than the hole. Parts slide or turn. Examples: H7/g6 for sliding parts, H8/f7 for running fits.
  • Transition: depending on the actual sizes, there may be a small gap or a small grip. Used for accurate location of parts that can still be taken apart, such as H7/k6.
  • Interference: the shaft is always larger than the hole. Parts are pressed together or assembled with heat. Example: H7/p6.

What these fits mean at 40 mm

FitTypeRange at Ø40 mm
H7/g6Clearance0.009 to 0.050 mm clearance
H7/h6Clearance0 to 0.041 mm clearance
H7/k6Transition0.023 mm clearance to 0.018 mm interference
H7/p6Interference0.001 to 0.042 mm interference

Choosing a fit

  • Bearings: follow the bearing maker's recommendation for the shaft and housing, which depends on load and rotation.
  • Locating pins and dowels: transition or light interference fits.
  • Gears and pulleys on keyed shafts: often transition fits, with the key carrying the torque.
  • Bushes pressed into housings: interference fits.
